Accepted paper checklist

  • Upload your camera-ready paper by November 7 (AoE). Use the workshop author portal on OpenReview.
  • Prepare for the poster session. Four selected papers also have five-minute student spotlight talks; see the program for the speaking order.
  • Meet us in Room 205, JW Marriott Austin. The workshop is on November 12, 2:00–6:00 PM CST.

This workshop is non-archival. Submission format and review guidelines are preserved below for reference.

Submission guidelines

Submission format

  • Appendices are allowed. We generally expect submissions to be 2–4 pages, excluding references, without an appendix. If an appendix is particularly important for your work, you may include one. We encourage keeping the main paper self-contained within the four-page limit.
  • Use the standard two-column IEEE conference paper template (LaTeX or Word).
  • New results, work in progress, benchmark proposals, position papers, open problems, negative results, and failure analyses are welcome.
  • Submit through the workshop venue on OpenReview.
  • Submission deadline: October 15, 2026 (AoE), extended from October 8. Notification: October 31, 2026. Camera-ready: November 7, 2026 (AoE).

Review process

  • Review will be single-blind: reviewers will see author identities, while reviewer identities will remain anonymous to authors.
  • Each submission will receive at least two reviews from the program committee.
  • Selection will emphasize relevance, clarity, novelty, technical quality, and potential to stimulate discussion.

Presentation and recognition

  • Accepted papers will be presented as posters, with a subset selected for short spotlight talks.
  • This workshop is non-archival. Accepted papers will be posted on this website.
  • A best workshop paper will be announced during the closing session.
  • Selected authors may be invited to contribute to the post-workshop open-problems report.

Topics of Interest

We welcome work on the following topics and related problems in contact-rich loco-manipulation.

Contact choices

Modes, transitions, and affordances

How robots represent, select, and switch between grasping, pushing, dragging, bracing, pivoting, pinning, and environment-assisted contact.

Whole-body control

Forceful interaction while moving

Locomotion and manipulation policies that coordinate balance, mobility, contact forces, dexterity, and recovery for legged, humanoid, and mobile robots.

Sensing

Contact and object state estimation

Fusion of vision, proprioception, tactile sensing, force feedback, and action history to reason about slip, mass, friction, geometry, and hidden state.

Learning

Structured policies and representations

Action spaces, latent contact abstractions, planner-generated data, foundation models, and hybrid model-based or learning-based approaches.

Transfer

Generalization across bodies and tasks

Methods that transfer across quadrupeds, humanoids, wheeled bases, arms, hands, object families, terrain, and hardware constraints.

Evaluation

Benchmarks, metrics, and failure modes

Datasets, tasks, protocols, negative results, and metrics for robustness, safety, contact efficiency, energy, recoverability, and sim-to-real transfer.

Who Should Submit

We welcome researchers working on robot learning, contact-rich manipulation, legged locomotion, humanoids, mobile manipulation, whole-body control, perception, planning, simulation, benchmarks, and real-world deployment. Late-stage PhD students, postdoctoral researchers, and others early in their careers are especially encouraged to submit work in progress and join the discussion.
